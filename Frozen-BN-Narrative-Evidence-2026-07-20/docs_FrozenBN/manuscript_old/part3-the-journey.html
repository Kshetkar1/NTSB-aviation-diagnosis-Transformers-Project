Part 3 — The Journey: What We Tried First and Why We Switched

Research is not a straight line, and this repository honestly records the detours: each major attempt lives in its own dated folder, with its status declared in PROJECT_MAP.md at the repository root. This part walks the timeline and — most importantly — explains why each earlier idea lost to the next one. Understanding the rejections is understanding the design.

3.1 March 2026 — Embedding similarity + counting (Embedding-Similarity-Counting-Path-2026-03-25/, SUPERSEDED)

The first idea was the simplest thing that could possibly work: embed all historical narratives, embed a new narrative, find its nearest neighbors by cosine similarity, and count outcomes among the neighbors — "17 of your 25 most similar accidents had substantial damage." Clustering (k-means) grouped similar accidents for browsing.

Why it was superseded. Counting neighbors answers exactly one question at a time and produces frequencies with no model behind them. There is no way to combine the counts with other knowledge ("we also know there was a fire"), no way to ask what-if questions, no validated probabilistic structure — and nothing about it is publishable as new methodology, because k-NN outcome counting is decades old. What survived: the embedding + retrieval machinery itself (shared/code/main_app.py) became the soft-evidence engine of the final approach, and the counting idea survives inside it as the k-NN severity readout (Part 4). Nothing was wasted; it just wasn't enough on its own.

3.2 June 2026 — Reproduce Zhang exactly (Zhang-Replication-Foundation-2026-06-04/, FOUNDATION)

Covered in Part 2. The decision that everything after would be anchored to a peer-reviewed, validated network — rather than to a model of our own invention — was made here.

3.3 June 2026 — Try to improve the network's probability tables (BN-CPT-Upgrade-Experiments-2026-06-19/, REJECTED)

With the replication in hand, the natural itch: can we make Zhang's CPTs better? Experiments included sparse CPT estimation (many parent-state combinations have very few example accidents, so their probability estimates are noisy) and uncertainty envelopes around the CPT entries.

Why it was rejected. A strategic contradiction, not a technical failure: every modification to the network breaks the sentence "the network we query is the one that was validated." If we retune the CPTs, we own the burden of re-validating the whole model — which is a different, much larger paper. The experiments taught us where the network's estimates are fragile (useful for the limitations section), and the variance-envelope machinery survives in Frozen-BN-Narrative-Evidence-2026-07-20/tests/bn_variance_envelope.py and the report outputs/BN_VARIANCE_REPORT.md. But the main line pivoted to the opposite philosophy: change nothing; add evidence. That pivot is where "frozen" was born.

3.4 July 2026 — The LLM-first parser (LLM-Narrative-Parser-Experiments-2026-07-15/, REJECTED as primary)

To get narrative facts into the network you must first extract them. The obvious modern answer: ask a large language model to read the narrative and output the network-node facts directly.

Why it was rejected as the primary path. Three independent reasons, each sufficient: (1) Accuracy — on the held-out evaluation the LLM-driven predictors reach roughly 69% injury / 52–58% damage, far below the 90.9% / 77.4% of the retrieval-based chain (measured under the leak-safe protocol: run the main evaluation with --llm gpt-4.1 --tag _llm; outputs in outputs/*_llm.json). (2) Determinism — LLM outputs vary run to run, which poisons reproducibility; a reviewer who reruns your pipeline must get your numbers. (3) Cost and dependency — every narrative costs API calls, and the method's core would depend on a black box we cannot version. What survived: the LLM remains as an optional tier-2 fallback reader in the combined parser (code/llm_evidence.py, invoked through code/query_to_bn.py) for narratives the deterministic parser cannot handle — with its extracted facts assigned data-measured strengths, never trusted raw. The tiered design: deterministic first (176 of 296 narratives), LLM fallback second (120), hard failure never (0).
The takeaway that shaped the paper. "The LLM is a text reader, not the predictive signal." The signal lives in the historical data (via retrieval); the LLM is one optional way to read text, and the system works better with the boring, auditable reader in front. This sentence — verified by ablation — is what makes the approach defensible in a reliability journal skeptical of LLM magic.

3.5 July 20, 2026 — Frozen BN + narrative evidence (Frozen-BN-Narrative-Evidence-2026-07-20/, ACCEPTED)

The synthesis of everything above: keep Zhang's network frozen (3.2/3.3), use embedding retrieval as the evidence engine (3.1), use the deterministic parser with optional LLM fallback as the text reader (3.4), and connect narrative to network through Pearl's virtual-evidence mechanism. Part 4 explains the machinery in full.

3.6 July 25, 2026 — Structural mapping / re-ranking (Structural-Mapping-Retrieval-2026-07-25/, EXPLORATORY, not in the main evaluation)

A refinement attempt on retrieval: instead of ranking historical accidents purely by embedding similarity, re-rank them by how well their coded structure (events, findings) matches what the parser extracted from the query narrative.

Why it is not in the main path. The added complexity did not buy a reliable improvement over plain cosine retrieval on the held-out tasks, and every additional moving part is another thing to validate and explain. It is kept as an appendix lane with worked examples — the kind of "we tried the obvious refinement; here is why the simple version stands" material reviewers actually like.

3.7 The pattern in all the rejections

AttemptFatal flawWhat survived into the final approach
Embedding + countingNo model, no reasoning, not novel The entire retrieval engine; k-NN severity readout
CPT upgradesBreaks the validation transfer The "frozen" philosophy; fragility knowledge for limitations
LLM-first parsingWeaker, non-deterministic, costly Tier-2 fallback reader with measured strengths
Structural re-rankingComplexity without measured benefit Appendix worked examples
The meta-lesson. Every rejection was decided by a measurement, not a preference — and each rejected folder still exists so the measurement can be rerun. When Part 5 shows the final numbers, remember that four alternative designs lost to this one on evidence.
Verify it yourself. The tier usage and LLM-vs-deterministic comparison regenerate with: 
export OPENAI_API_KEY=...   # only needed for the LLM arm
python Frozen-BN-Narrative-Evidence-2026-07-20/tests/frozenbn_heldout_narrative_bn_eval.py --llm gpt-4.1 --tag _llm
Outputs land in outputs/heldout_narrative_bn_eval_llm.json (accuracy per predictor, tier counts) without touching the canonical leak-safe results — the --tag flag exists precisely so ablations can never overwrite headline numbers.
