Part 9 — Advisor Q&A: What Maha and Jesse Will Ask

Source: docs_FrozenBN/CRIB_SHEET.md (2026-07-29 numbers). Each block gives the question, why it is hard, what not to say, and a recommended answer in plain language.

Jesse #1 "How does the narrative get into the Bayesian network?"

Three routes, all ending as evidence on named nodes:

  1. Hard evidence — parser matches NTSB vocabulary ("loss of engine power", "fire"). Node set to Yes, p=1. No model.
  2. Soft evidence — embed narrative, top-100 similar 1982–2006 accidents; strength = fraction of neighbors with that code (e.g. 40/100 → 0.40).
  3. Virtual evidence (bn-sev) — neighbor injury/damage distribution on severity nodes (Pearl's Jeffrey conditioning).

One line: The narrative is translated into evidence on Zhang's own coded variables; every strength is a fraction counted from 1982–2006 data.

Do not say: "asserted", "the model learned", or call retrieval output a "posterior."

Jesse #2 "What are the weights? Are they learned?"

No learned weights in the primary chain. Numbers are (a) frozen Zhang CPTs, (b) measured retrieval fractions, or (c) fixed defaults (top_k=100, chosen once on 2002–2006 validation). Only the three supervised baselines (lr, emb-lr, tfidf-lr) are fitted. Inventory: FREE_PARAMETERS.md.

Jesse #3 "So what are you training? Where's the validation set?"

Nothing is trained. 1982–2006 builds the network and index; 2002–2006 picks top_k once; 2007–2019 is held out for final scoring only. Say frozen, never trained.

Jesse #4 — the hard one "How many times have you looked at that held-out set?"

Concede immediately. No parameters were fitted to 2007–2019, but we scored the 296 repeatedly while fixing leakage and designing ablations — so decisions saw held-out results. Selection optimism is possible.

The real fix is a one-shot run on 2020–2024 we have never touched. That is a prerequisite for submission, not a nice-to-have. Until then, read numbers as developed-on-test. See RESULTS §5.6.

Jesse #5 "What exactly is that 93% — sensitivity? specificity?"

Old 93%/81% are superseded. Leak-safe headline:

  • Accuracy (top-1 of 4 states): injury 90.9%, damage 77.4% (n=296, bn-sev)
  • Macro-F1: 0.470 injury / 0.697 damage
  • Severe-outcome screen: injury 93.5% sens / 96.8% spec; damage 75.3% / 89.8%

Full table: outputs/heldout_significance.md.

Jesse #6 "Isn't the model reading the outcome in the text?"

It was a real risk; we closed it. Outcome phrases stripped before any embedding or parsing; stated-severity evidence OFF by default. Numbers dropped 93%/81% → 90.9%/77.4%. Redaction removes explicit outcome statements, not mechanism signal — stall/turbulence wording still predicts severity legitimately. We promoted the redaction leak probe to baseline (tfidf-lr) so we cannot hide it.

Maha & Jesse "What is the BN contributing?"

On held-out accuracy, the BN matches retrieval by construction (0/296 discordant) — raw signal is narrative→neighbors. The BN adds: (1) validated causal structure (Zhang reproduced to the digit); (2) joint reasoning across nodes; (3) what-if and per-node explanations. Retrieval is the accuracy claim; the BN is the reasoning-and-explanation claim. Do not oversell the reverse.

Jesse / Maha "Does the BN do cross-node inference on held-out data?"

No on fire. Parsed event evidence only → P(fire): BN AUC ~0.38–0.46; retrieval ~0.97; regex ~0.94. Narrow claim: what-if and evidence composition on a frozen auditable model — not held-out predictive lift on unobserved nodes. Diagnosis event path (57.7%) shows parsed facts reach cause nodes; that does not extend to fire. Table: outputs/fire_node_cross_inference.md.

Maha "Do your numbers match Zhang's?"

Yes on published quantities: 102 fire occurrences, P(fire)=102/184,517,128=5.527942e-7, 85/85 Table 7 rows. "113/113" is an internal consistency check, not the paper comparison. Upgraded BN: 77/93 posteriors exact or close on published Table 8/9 numbers. Reports in ZHANG_REPRODUCTION_REPORT.md.

Maha "How do you evaluate diagnosis across the 2008 coding change?"

Roll both eras to four CICTT top-level categories (Personnel / Aircraft / Environment / Organizational). Legacy mapped by published keyword rules (97.6% coverage). Do NOT call it an audit — say "documented rule set with ≤2 pp sensitivity bound on contested rows." Results (n=253): retrieval 84.2%, emb-LR 88.1%, BN event 57.7%, baseline 45.8%.

Jesse "A linear model beats you — why is your architecture justified?"

We ran that attack ourselves on the same 296 accidents. Severity: emb-LR 91.6%/74.0%, TF-IDF 92.2%/73.3% vs our 90.9%/77.4% — no significant difference (Holm p ≥ 0.29). We win on damage Macro-F1 and severe-damage sensitivity. Diagnosis: emb-LR beats us by 3.9 pp — we disclose it. Selling point: zero training, every prediction decomposes into named evidence on a validated network.
