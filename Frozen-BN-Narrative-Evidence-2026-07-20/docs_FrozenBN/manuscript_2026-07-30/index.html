What this project is, in three sentences

Zhang & Mahadevan (2021) built a Bayesian network — a probabilistic model of how aviation accidents unfold — from NTSB's hand-coded accident records for 1982–2006; we reproduce that network, freeze it (never retrain it), and verify it against every probability he published, so the model we query is provably the validated one. The new knowledge is about the narratives: a free-text accident description is converted into evidence the frozen network understands, and one propagation updates every node at once — downstream severity (prognosis) and upstream causes (diagnosis) — with zero training. Both directions are scored on held-out 2007–2019 accidents: severity reaches 90.9% injury / 77.4% damage and cause categories reach 84.2% by narrative retrieval, statistically tied with supervised models — the contribution is the leak-safe evidence interface and honest parity, not better accuracy.

What this project does not claim. The network does not improve predictive accuracy on severity (0/296 discordant between bn-sev and retrieval-sev). TF-IDF logistic regression is numerically best on injury (92.2% vs 90.9%, Holm p = 0.87). On diagnosis, emb-LR beats retrieval by 3.9 points (88.1% vs 84.2%). The fire-node experiment shows the BN does not beat retrieval for cross-node fire prediction (AUC ~0.38–0.46 vs ~0.97). What the frozen network buys is joint reasoning, what-if queries, and per-node explanations on a validated causal structure — at zero severity accuracy cost.

The parts (read in order)

Part 1

Background and vocabulary

NTSB data, Bayesian networks, CPTs, priors, posteriors, evidence, embeddings, held-out testing, leakage — explained from zero with inline definitions before every technical term.

Part 2

Reproducing Zhang: the foundation

Why we reproduced the 2021 paper to the digit first: 102 fire accidents, P(fire) = 5.527942×10⁻⁷, 85/85 Table 7 rows, and the 85-vs-113 honesty lesson.

Part 3

The journey: what we tried first and why we switched

Embedding-counting, CPT upgrades, LLM-first parsing, structural re-ranking — each superseded or rejected, with dated folders as receipts.

Part 4

The pipeline: how a narrative becomes evidence

Redaction → deterministic parser (hard evidence) → retrieval (soft evidence) → k-NN severity (virtual evidence) → inference. Why bn-fused double-counts and collapses.

Part 5

Evaluating severity (prognosis)

Held-out design, metrics (accuracy, Macro-F1, Brier, McNemar, Holm, bootstrap CIs, sensitivity/specificity), baselines, and the full results table.

Part 6

Evaluating diagnosis across a taxonomy break

The 2008 coding change, era-fair four-category rollup, mapping sensitivity bound (not an "audit"), and diagnosis results including negative results we report on purpose.

Part 7

Leakage, audits, and honest claims

93%/81% → 90.9%/77.4%, redaction scope, leak probe promoted to baseline, fire-node negative result, limitations, and the 2020–2024 confirmatory gate.
