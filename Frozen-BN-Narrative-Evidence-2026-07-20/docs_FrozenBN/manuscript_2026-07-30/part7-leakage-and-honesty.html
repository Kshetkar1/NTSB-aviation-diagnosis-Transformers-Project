Part 7 — Leakage, Audits, and Honest Claims

This part is the reason the other six can be believed. It tells the story of how the project's numbers got worse on purpose — from 93%/81% to 90.9%/77.4% — and why the worse numbers are the publishable ones. If you learn one research habit from this manuscript, learn this part's.

7.1 The discovery: our model was reading the answer

What went wrong. The early severity results were 93% injury / 81% damage — suspiciously high. The suspicion was correct: NTSB narratives often state the outcome in plain text ("the airplane was destroyed"; "the flight attendant was seriously injured"), and both the retrieval embedding and a "stated severity" reader were consuming those sentences. The model was partially copying, not predicting. The fix had three parts: (1) a redaction pass (redact_severity_phrases in code/query_to_bn.py) that strips stated injury/damage phrases, death/medical wording, and NTSB's full-report boilerplate before any use of the text; (2) the stated-severity reader demoted to an OFF-by-default ablation (LEAK_SAFE_SEVERITY in shared/code/config.py); (3) a rerun of everything. Numbers moved to 90.9% / 77.4% — and every document now labels the old numbers "pre-fix, superseded."
Why we treat losing 2–4 points as a win. Because 93/81 was indefensible — one sharp reviewer question ("does the text state the outcome?") would have collapsed it. 90.9/77.4 survives that question with an audit trail. Honest numbers are the ones you can stand next to.

7.2 What redaction does and does not do — precision matters

The precise claim. Redaction removes explicit outcome statements. It does NOT remove outcome predictability — a narrative about severe turbulence injuring a flight attendant still predicts a serious-injury code from mechanism wording alone, and that is the legitimate signal every predictor uses. Early drafts blurred this distinction; the current documents state it exactly (README.md, leakage protocol section; docs_FrozenBN/RESULTS_SECTION.md).

The audit that measures this is tests/redaction_leak_probe.py — two probes:

ProbeDesignInjury (full → redacted)Damage (full → redacted)
Window-trained (the clean audit) TF-IDF + logistic regression fit ONLY on 1982–2006 narratives, scored once on the 296. 92.2% → 92.2%75.7% → 73.3%
In-sample CV (worst-case bound) 5-fold cross-validation on the held-out text itself. 89.5% → 89.2%74.0% → 72.3%

The token check is the point: after redaction, the probe's most predictive words are mechanism words (turbulence, flight attendant, landing gear, postcrash fire) — not outcome words. That is what "the leak is closed; the signal is legitimate" looks like as a measurement. Report: outputs/redaction_leak_probe.md.

7.3 The probe that became a baseline — an honesty story

What we almost got wrong. Look at that probe table again: 92.2% injury. That is higher than bn-sev's 90.9% — meaning the strongest injury predictor in the repository was sitting in a leak-audit file while the paper's baseline table showed only weaker competitors. Nothing was computed wrong; the placement was wrong, and a reviewer finding it would reasonably suspect burial. The fix: the probe was promoted to a first-class baseline (tests/tfidf_lr_baseline_heldout.py, configuration unchanged so it cannot be accused of tuning), scored per-item on the identical cohort, and added to the Holm primary family. Result (Part 5): a statistical tie (p = 0.87 injury / 0.29 damage; 8 discordant accidents), TF-IDF best on injury, bn-sev best on damage and on damage Macro-F1 and severe-screen. The story survived being honest — and is stronger for it, because now the claim "accuracy is a commodity; the network's value is reasoning" has three supervised witnesses instead of two.

Why the mapping row is deliberately not called an audit. Every other row in that table measures a fact: an ID either is or is not in the index, a probe either does or does not recover outcome words. The category mapping is different — it is a judgement about taxonomy, and the only defensible check on a judgement is a coder who is not you. Our 75-row review pass was an AI assistant with the rows then checked by the first author, which is self-review with extra steps; calling that an "audit" or "dual coding" would be an overclaim, so we do not. What we publish instead is the bound (Part 6.2): the contested rows are 6.2% of the mapped mass, they move absolute accuracy by at most 2 points in the same direction for every predictor, and every ordering the paper claims survives that by more than an order of magnitude. The rules themselves are two short keyword functions in the repository, so a reader who disagrees with a specific rule can change it and recompute.

7.5 The limitations we disclose (because no audit can remove them)

LimitationWhy it existsHow the paper handles it
Test-set reuse The 296 held-out accidents were scored repeatedly during development (protocol fixes, ablation design), so reported accuracy may carry some selection optimism — no model parameter was ever fitted to them, but decisions saw their results. Disclosed in RESULTS_SECTION.md; the clean fix is a one-shot confirmation run on a never-touched window (2020–2024), named as planned confirmatory work.
Embedding pretraining The OpenAI embedding model may have seen 2007–2019 NTSB reports during its own training; no redaction can reach inside it. Disclosed as a limitation; comparisons stay fair because emb-LR shares the channel, and the TF-IDF baseline (85.5/60.1 → for parsed LR; 92.2/73.3 for TF-IDF text) is the pretraining-free reference point.
Rare classes 3 fatal, 16 minor-injury, 25 Organizational-cause accidents — too few to learn or retrieve reliably. Per-class recall and confusion matrices published; the fatal-injury misses stated plainly (Part 5.5), and the Organizational best case (1 of 25, by the BN event path) stated rather than rounded to "none".
Retrospective text Narratives are written after investigation — this is not real-time prediction. Framed as triage/coding-assist/what-if analysis throughout (Part 1.3).
The network adds no severity accuracy By construction, not by accident: bn-sev and retrieval-sev are identical on all 296 accidents (0 discordant), because the network faithfully mediates the k-NN evidence rather than adding information to it. Stated in the results, the README, and Part 5 — the contribution claimed is the reasoning layer at zero accuracy cost, never predictive lift.
Four-category diagnosis granularity The 2008 taxonomy break means legacy and CICTT codes share no vocabulary below CICTT's top level, so the rollup to four categories is forced by the data, and the rollup itself rests on a keyword rule set that no independent coder has checked. Rules published in full; influence bounded at ≤ 2 pp, order-preserving (Part 6.2). Nothing finer than category level is scored, and Part 6.5 says so.
Residual disagreement with Zhang 12 of the 93 published BN numbers still differ after the network upgrades (48 exact, 29 close). Each is attributed, not hidden: a build-variance envelope quantifies how much tie-jitter in Zhang's own construction moves cells, and his released NTSB.xdsl does not reproduce his published Table 8 either (outputs/bn_posterior_parity.json).

7.7 The habit to take away

The pattern behind every fix in this part. Each problem was found by asking "what would a hostile reviewer check first?" and then running that check ourselves, before they could. Leakage → probe it. Baseline fairness → build the attacker. Judgement calls you cannot independently verify (the category mapping) → bound how much they could possibly matter, and say plainly that a bound is not a validation. Prose claims → recompute them from the per-item files (that is how a false "all 3 fatal accidents are flagged severe" sentence was caught — the raw data said 1 of 3). The discipline generalizes far beyond this project: your results are only as strong as the strongest attack they have already survived.
