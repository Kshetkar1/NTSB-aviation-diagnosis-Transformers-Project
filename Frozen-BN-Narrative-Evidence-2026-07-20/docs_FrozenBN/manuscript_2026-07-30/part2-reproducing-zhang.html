Part 2 — Reproducing Zhang: the Foundation

2.1 Why reproduce someone else's paper before doing anything new?

Why we did this. Our entire claim is "a validated network can be driven by free text." The word validated does the heavy lifting: if the network we query is not demonstrably the one Zhang & Mahadevan published and peer review accepted, then every downstream result sits on an unverified model, and a reviewer can dismiss all of it with one question. Reproduction-first also forced us to understand every data-filtering decision in the original paper — knowledge that later saved us from several subtle mistakes. The reproduction is the boring part of the project and also the load-bearing part.

Everything reproduced lives in Zhang-Replication-Foundation-2026-06-04/ (the vendored reference material: Zhang's network file NTSB.xdsl, the Bureau of Transportation Statistics departure spreadsheet, replication scripts) and in Frozen-BN-Narrative-Evidence-2026-07-20/tests/reproduce_*.py. The complete claim audit is Frozen-BN-Narrative-Evidence-2026-07-20/docs_FrozenBN/ZHANG_REPRODUCTION_REPORT.md.

2.2 What Zhang built, in plain language

Zhang & Mahadevan (RESS 2021) took NTSB's coded records for large-aircraft accidents from 1982–2006 and built a Bayesian network for accident analysis, with in-flight fire as the running example. Three published quantities matter for reproduction:

QuantityZhang publishedWe reproduceStatus
Fire occurrences, 1982–2006102102exact
Prior P(fire) per flight5.53×10⁻⁷5.527942×10⁻⁷exact (his rounding)
Table 7 — cause distribution given fire85 rows85/85 match exact
Vocabulary — P(fire) is a formula, not network output. Zhang computes the fire prior as (number of fire accidents) ÷ (total US departures in the window, from the BTS spreadsheet: 184,517,128 flights). We reproduce that arithmetic to the digit. We are careful to say we reproduce his formula, not a marginal propagated through his network — an early draft of our report blurred that distinction and we corrected it (docs_FrozenBN/ZHANG_REPRODUCTION_REPORT.md, executive summary).

2.3 Our dataset window

To rebuild his numbers we had to rebuild his data. The result is shared/data/processed/refined_dataset_1982_2006.json: 1,742 accidents, of which 1,286 carry a usable factual narrative (≥100 characters) and 102 are fire accidents. Every count in the project traces back to this file.

What went wrong along the way (and the fix). NTSB distributes its pre-2008 data with a legacy "sequence of events" layer in separate tables. Our first merged dataset never joined that layer, so all 1,742 pre-2007 accidents initially had empty findings — which silently broke any cause analysis. The fix (re-merging the legacy layer) is documented in docs_FrozenBN/ZHANG_REPRODUCTION_REPORT.md and implemented by tests/add_findings_1982_2006.py and tests/rebuild_1982_2006.py. Lesson an undergrad should take: the hardest bugs in applied work are usually in the data plumbing, not the model.

2.4 The 85-versus-113 lesson: how a true number becomes a false claim

What went wrong. Zhang's Table 7 lists causes for fire accidents, filtered to findings coded as Cause or Factor ("C/F filter"), which yields 85 rows — and we match all 85 exactly. But we also had an extended mode without that filter, which yields 113 causes, and for a while our documents claimed "113/113 match Zhang exactly." That claim was wrong in a subtle way: Zhang never published a 113-row table, so "matching" it only meant our own two code paths agreed with each other — internal consistency dressed up as external validation. During the July 2026 review pass we corrected every document to say 85/85 against the published table, with the 113 figure explicitly labeled an internal consistency check. The correction touched docs_FrozenBN/ZHANG_REPRODUCTION_REPORT.md, README.md, the crib sheet, and three presentation decks.
Why this story is in the manuscript. Because it is the project's clearest example of the difference between a true number and an honest claim. Nothing about the 113 was computationally wrong — the sentence around it overclaimed. Most of the review work in this project (Part 7) is exactly this kind of sentence surgery, and you should learn to spot it in your own writing.

2.5 What "frozen" means, precisely

Vocabulary — frozen. After the network is built from the 1982–2006 coded data (structure + every CPT), nothing about it ever changes again. New accidents never update its tables. All adaptation to a new accident happens at query time, through evidence. Freezing is what makes the validation transferable: the thing we query is provably the thing that was validated. It is also what makes the approach cheap — no retraining, ever.

The freeze boundary in code: code/bn_upgraded.py builds the network once from prognosis.load_dataset() (the window file); the evaluation scripts then only call inference on it. The complete inventory of what is and is not fitted anywhere in the system: docs_FrozenBN/FREE_PARAMETERS.md.

Verify it yourself. 
python Frozen-BN-Narrative-Evidence-2026-07-20/tests/reproduce_fire_prior.py
python Frozen-BN-Narrative-Evidence-2026-07-20/tests/reproduce_table7.py
The first prints the fire count (102) and P(fire) = 5.527942×10⁻⁷ from the BTS departures file in Zhang-Replication-Foundation-2026-06-04/reference/data/. The second regenerates the Table 7 comparison. Two bugs we found and fixed in these very scripts during review — a wrong departures-file path and a transposed digit in the total-flights constant (184,572,128 vs the correct 184,517,128) — are worth knowing about: even verification code needs verification.
