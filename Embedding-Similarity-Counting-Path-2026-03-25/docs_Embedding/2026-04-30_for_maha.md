## SLIDE 8 — Why the numbers differ — two ways of computing probability (~120 sec)

**Zhang's Bayesian Network asks:**
> *"Given this evidence, under my parametric model with conditional probability tables, what's the most likely outcome?"*
>
> → produces sharp, concentrated answers (P = 0.95)

**Our retrieval system asks:**
> *"Of 50 historical NTSB incidents most similar to this query, how often did each cause appear?"*
>
> → produces diffuse, frequency-like estimates (P = 0.10)

**Both are valid. They're answering different questions.**

The 28% gap between us and Zhang isn't a bug to fix — it's the difference in **what each system is designed to mean by "probability."**

**Say:**
> "If you remember nothing else from the talk, remember this slide. The two systems are not the same kind of thing. Zhang's network is parametric — it has conditional probability tables that were learned to give sharp answers under hypothetical evidence. Our system is empirical retrieval — it pulls historical cases and reports what happened in those cases. So when Zhang says 95%, that's a confidence statement under his model. When we say 9%, that's basically saying 'in the most similar fifty incidents, about five of them had this cause.' Different question, different answer.
>
> The reason that's interesting and not just a problem is on the next slide."

*(This is the second-most-important slide. If Maha gets it, the rest of the talk lands. If she doesn't, slow down and use the "frequency vs. parametric" framing again.)*

## Backup slides (only if asked)

### B1 — Why structural mapping had a small effect

The structural reweighting multiplies the retrieval score by `exp(α × structural_similarity)`. We used **α = 0.5**, which gives a maximum +65% boost on a perfectly matching candidate. Since boosts apply across all 50 retrieved neighbors and probabilities are normalized, the effect on final probabilities is roughly 0.001 on average. That's enough to flip one argmax in 77 incidents but not enough to reshape distributions.

If asked: "would a higher α make A2 closer to Zhang?" → No. The reweighting can change rankings, but cannot reshape probability mass the way a parametric BN does. The gap is paradigm, not parameter.

### B2 — The 13 unmappable causes (sample)

- *Personnel issues — Physical — Alertness/Fatigue — Flight crew*
- *Aircraft handling — Towing and taxiing — Towing*
- *Personnel issues — Physical — Ground crew*
- *Personnel issues — Action/decision — Maintenance personnel*

These are real NTSB causes from the held-out test set. None of them fit Zhang's 54-code occurrence scheme.
