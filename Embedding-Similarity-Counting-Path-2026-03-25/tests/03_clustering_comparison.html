Clustering Method Comparison: K-Means vs GMM

Both methods use K = 50 clusters on the same dataset of 1,817 NTSB incident embeddings.
Embeddings are PCA-reduced to 100 dimensions (explains 71.6% of variance) for a fair, numerically stable comparison.
All metrics are computed in the same PCA-100 space.

GMM assigns each incident a probability distribution over all 50 clusters. Entropy measures how uncertain these assignments are. Low entropy = the model is confident. High entropy = the incident could belong to many clusters.

Verdict

K-Means wins 3–0 on standard cluster quality metrics.
K-Means is the stronger choice for this dataset. It also has the practical advantage of hard cluster assignments, which plug directly into the Law of Total Probability used in the Bayesian diagnosis framework.
Note on methodology: K-Means in production runs on full 1,536-dim unit-normalized embeddings (equivalent to spherical K-Means optimizing cosine similarity). GMM requires PCA reduction for numerical stability in high dimensions. Both are evaluated here in the same PCA-100 space for a fair metric comparison. The slight disadvantage this introduces for K-Means means the production K-Means performance is likely at least as good as shown here.
